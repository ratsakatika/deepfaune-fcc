Notes & method

Unit of analysis. Consecutive frames sharing a sequence number within a folder form one photographic sequence (burst); each sequence counts as one detection event, since burst frames are not independent records of separate visits. Events are grouped by sequence_id; if the CSV predates that column, the folder path and seqnum are used instead.

Filtering. Every panel reflects the current filters: predictions are re-derived from the per-class score_* columns, so thresholds and species exclusions apply retrospectively and reversibly to the whole archive. An event scoring below the threshold is labelled ‘undefined’ and appears in the unidentified group.

Scope. The default view is the official systematic survey: images in the drive’s Camera Trap Monitoring/Collections folder. The All images toggle additionally counts off-protocol frames recorded at the same cells. Files in the drive’s recycle bin are excluded from both views.

Caveats. Labels are automated predictions and are not field-verified; the ‘unidentified’ group holds below-threshold events and clusters in daylight, consistent with empty or vegetation triggers. The seasonal panel is not effort-corrected. Night bands are nominal, not computed from local sunrise/sunset. Drilldowns and the clock for sparse groups rest on few events and are descriptive only.

Place. The study area is the Făgăraș Mountains (Southern Carpathians, Romania); the study-area code FAR denotes Făgăraș. The cameras lie around , with coordinates and setup dates taken from the field protocol. The survey is a fixed grid re-deployed across years: every camera is placed by matching grid number and ignoring the year suffix.

Field protocol

How this page works

This page reads the master results file in your browser (nothing is uploaded) and rebuilds the whole dashboard from whatever filters you set. Because the classifier's raw score for every species is recorded for every image, predictions are re-derived on the spot: thresholds and species choices apply retrospectively to the whole archive, reversibly, without reclassifying anything.

The filters

  • Confidence threshold - an event whose best species score is below this is labelled ‘undefined’ instead of a species name. The companion tick box decides whether those undefined events stay in the results (shown in red in the table, and in the grey ‘unidentified’ group in the charts) or are dropped entirely; they are dropped by default.
  • Sequence gap - the rule that chains photographs into events: each photo within this many seconds of the previous one (in the same folder) joins the same event, so an event can last far longer than the gap when an animal lingers. Blank keeps the grouping baked in at classification; entering a number regroups the whole archive by re-reading the file, so Generate takes about as long as the original load. The per-species gap override boxes in the species table go further: repeat detections of that species at the same camera within the override merge into one event (an independence interval), re-scored from the combined photographs.
  • Scope - the official survey is the drive's Camera Trap Monitoring/Collections folder; ‘All images’ adds everything else on the drive. Out-of-scope events are counted in ‘Events filtered out’, which is why that figure is large even with every other filter relaxed.
  • Species tick boxes - an unticked species can never be named: events whose best guess was that species fall to their next-best candidate. Scores are always the model's confidence measured against all species it knows, so unticking species never inflates the survivors' scores, and ticking more species can only ever name more events, never fewer. Ticking a single species answers ‘how sure was the model it was X?’ for every animal event - expect very few to pass a high threshold.

The numbers

  • Events filtered out - everything the current filters removed, with the reasons listed under the figure: outside the chosen scope, recycle-bin files, excluded categories (empty / human / vehicle / below-threshold).
  • An event is one photographic sequence (burst), so bursts are counted once. sequence_id is a short code for the folder plus the sequence number, because the plain sequence number restarts at 1 in every folder and is not unique.
  • Events with an implausible date (a dead camera clock stamping 1970) are counted in the totals and station panels but left off the time charts.

The panels

  • The camera grid - each circle is a camera at its real position (the 2023 field protocol is built into this page; a protocol file can replace it). Choose a species to size the circles by its detections; tap a circle for detail.
  • Daily rhythm - detections by hour of day; midnight at the top, the shaded half is night. Only events with a usable time are counted.
  • Through the seasons - weekly timeline or by-month calendar, stacked by ecological group; click a group in the legend to split it into species.
  • What the cameras saw / Busiest cameras - species and station totals; click a group in the legend to hide or show it.
  • Events - every surviving event, oldest first, pageable, with filter boxes under each column heading (text matches anywhere; number boxes are minimums; these affect the table only). Click a row to preview its photographs when the image files are reachable from where the page is opened.

Saving results

  • Download events CSV - one row per surviving event, with the master file's column names (prediction_seq, top1_seq, score_seq, above_threshold...) plus second and third choices and event extras.
  • Download images CSV - re-reads the master and writes only the rows of surviving events, with the master's columns plus top2_seq/top3_seq and their scores beside top1_seq; the sequence-level verdict columns are refreshed to match the current filters.
  • Save species summary - species, group, events, images and mean confidence.
  • Each figure saves as an image (PNG, and SVG for the rhythm ring).

Sharing and phones

On the survey computer run cd ~/Desktop && python3 -m http.server 8000, then open http://<computer-ip>:8000/dashboard_and_filter.html on a phone on the same network: the master file loads automatically. The seasonal chart and the map background need an internet connection the first time; everything else works offline.
